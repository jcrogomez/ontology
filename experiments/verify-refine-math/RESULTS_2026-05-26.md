# Results — 2026-05-26 dry-run, Ollama arm

## Headline

The Ollama arm of H1 is **structurally infeasible on the current
hardware** (8 GB M-series Mac). Plumbing works end-to-end; the
cohort budget does not survive contact with measured per-call
latency.

## What we ran

Two smoke runs against `problems/usamo2026_p4.json`, both with the
same reduced config (`--accept-passes 1 --max-iterations 2
--reject-after-critical 3 --max-output-tokens 3000`).

| Model                | Verdict          | Wall-clock | Per-call avg | Output quality                                    |
| -------------------- | ---------------- | ---------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| `qwen2.5-coder:7b`   | reject (3 crit)  | 14 495 s   | ~30-44 min   | Long proofs, verifier returns `critical` w/ counts |
| `qwen2-math:1.5b`    | max_iterations   | 71 s       | 5-15 s       | Short, loses problem across iters; verifier `unclear` |

Traces in `runs/`:
- `usamo2026_p4__qwen2.5-coder:7b__20260526T053821Z.json`
- `usamo2026_p4__qwen2-math:1.5b__20260526T123541Z.json`

## What we learned

1. **Local 7B on 8 GB Mac is ~0.5 tok/s sustained**, not the 5-20 tok/s
   the README estimated. A pipeline run at upstream defaults
   (max-iter=30, accept-passes=5) extrapolates to **tens of hours per
   problem**; the cohort of 6 to weeks. HYPOTHESIS's 6 h ceiling is
   exceeded by a single smoke.
2. **The pipeline itself is correctly wired**: solver → self-improve
   → initial-verify → (correct → verify) loop runs end-to-end; the
   verifier discriminates (returns `critical` with specific error counts
   on qwen-coder, not a permissive accept).
3. **Going smaller (1.5B) trades the time problem for a competence
   problem**: qwen2-math:1.5b literally forgot the problem statement
   between step 1 (initial solve) and step 2 (self-improve) — its
   "improved" solution was for a completely different problem ("is n a
   perfect square?"). The verifier returned `unclear` because there
   was no judgment to make.
4. **H2 (verifier-honesty under accept) cannot be tested on either arm
   yet** — neither model produced an accept verdict, so the conditional
   sample is empty.

## Decision point

To make H1 actually testable, one of:

- **Hardware**: a machine that can drive a 7B-Q4 at ≥10 tok/s (60×
  speedup over current). That brings the cohort default config down
  from weeks to ~1 day. Rough thresholds: ≥24 GB unified memory on
  Apple Silicon, or any discrete-GPU box with ≥16 GB VRAM.
- **API tokens**: lift the Anthropic-arm hold from HYPOTHESIS §H1-anthropic.
  Sonnet 4.6 at ~80 tok/s would put the cohort at 2-4 h wall-clock,
  ~$10-30 in spend (well under the $50 ceiling). This also tests the
  *replication* the paper actually claims, against a frontier-tier
  model.

What does **not** work:
- Continuing on the current hardware with a smaller model. Below 7B
  the model is too weak to hold multi-turn problem state on Olympiad
  questions; the loop has nothing to lift.
- Cutting iterations / tokens further. The smoke already ran with
  aggressively reduced params; cutting more removes the loop's
  ability to refine at all.

## What is preserved for either path

- HYPOTHESIS.md remains the pre-registration. The Ollama dry-run
  outcome documented here is a **falsifier-adjacent measurement of
  feasibility, not of H1 itself** — H1-ollama on 7B is not refuted,
  only un-runnable on this box.
- The pipeline + cohort + grading scripts work as-is for either
  backend. Anthropic path needs only `--backend anthropic` + an
  `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
- The two smoke traces are useful as **structural anchors**: they
  capture how the verifier behaves on a competent-but-wrong 7B
  solution and on a too-weak 1.5B solution. Either can be reused as
  baselines against the Anthropic arm.
